Consult `references/sources.md` only when detailed creator examples or specialist guidance are needed.

# Research a decision

1. State the question, decision it supports, and scope.
   Check available local evidence before searching broadly.
2. Prefer official documentation, source code, standards, and original research.
   Verify changing facts against current sources and record the relevant version or date.
3. Separate source claims, observations from local execution, and your inference.
   Cite the exact supporting page or file.
   Check conflicting evidence rather than averaging incompatible claims.
4. When the question concerns runtime behavior, inspect the pinned dependency or run a small relevant experiment if authorized.
   A recommendation in a creator's skill is not proof of current library behavior.
5. Save a short note under the project's existing research convention when an artifact is useful.
   Include the question, findings, alternatives, recommendation, evidence, and remaining uncertainty.

Use a background researcher only when delegation is authorized.
Research does not authorize installing packages, publishing conclusions, or making the researched change.
